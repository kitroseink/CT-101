About Me

Bio

Michaela Barker (she/they) is an artist, designer, linguist and technology researcher. She is currently a senior undergraduate student at UC Santa Cruz studying Creative Technologies with a minor in Linguistics and a minor in Literature. Weaving in her background in visual design, they use interactive media to create, evaluate, and iterate technology into more thoughtful programs to provide environmental awareness and facilitate social justice interactions for users. Her core research interests lie in this intersection between the digital arts and humanities, particularly in application to the study of technology, computational linguistics, and digital media at the heart of Human Computer Interaction (HCI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP). Their extensive background as a visual artist and designer, has only been emphasized by her expertise in illustration, UI/UX design, motion graphics, and technical proficiencies in Python, CSS and HTML. Although she continues to work in the familiar mediums of digital painting, concept art and illustration, she has pushed her craft to learn 3D modeling and animation, creative coding and technical art workflows. After completing her undergraduate degree, Michaela aspires to create interactive stories and worlds through digital media that examines the boundaries of technology and the arts.

Artist Statement

From an early age, Michaela Barker has been a prolific artist and writer, having received recognition as 2020 recipient of the Lamorinda Visual Arts Award in high school. Following the exhibition of her illustration work in the Lamorinda Arts Council show, Michaela’s artwork was displayed in the Bank of America gallery in 2020. Moreover, her drawings were first published in the Girls Ghost Zine in 2021 as an initial contributor and were published twice more in recent editions. As an inaugural member of the Campolindo High School’s student-run journalism visual design department, Michaela created 12 illustrations, 3 covers and a t-shirt design within her junior year. In her senior year of high school, they served as Co-Visual Media editor of “The Claw,” leading a team of 12 illustrators, photographers and videographers while also continuing to produce multiple editorial illustration and graphic design works. Throughout her time at UCSC, she has enriched her artistic passions with a strong pursuit for interdisciplinary study. Initially a game design major, she took courses in computer science, discrete mathematics, and game design in her first year. In her second year, as she found herself more drawn to analyzing the evolution and impact of the technologies she was studying, she transitioned to linguistics with a computational focus and also began to venture more into studying literature and myth, both in tangent with digital spaces, and in contrast. Her illustration work can be found in publications such as the Chinquapin and Matchbox Literary magazines. During this time she also facilitated the development of data-mining and translation engine tooling for multilingual scholars as a research assistant in the AI Humanities Lab within the “Humanities in the Age of Artificial Intelligence Research” cluster. To continue her work on User Interfaces for translation engine tooling and researching the impact of AI technologies upon academia, Michaela Barker was selected as an Earth Futures Institute 2024-2025 Frontiers Fellow, where her fellowship thesis project was mentored by Professor of History, Minghui Hu, and Professor of Linguistics, Pranav Anand. In her third year, her desire to reconnect with her creative pursuits and further explore the lasting impacts of technology, led her to join the new Creative Technologies program. During this year she was also selected for the Chancellor's Undergraduate Internship Program in which she interned at the Center for Coastal Climate Resilience (CCCR) as a digital communications intern. At the CCCR, Michaela expanded her visual design skill set into video production by helping create a data story about the intricacies of data analysis for coastal flood modeling. Now a senior within the Creative Technologies program, Michaela has aimed to enrich her design practice as a technical artist and writer by making technology and critical media studies more accessible. Through rigorous design course work, she has improved her technical ability and confidence to develop multidisciplinary visual communication skills. Although she continues to work in the familiar mediums of digital painting, illustration and 2D animation, she has pushed her craft to learn 3D modeling and animation, creative coding and technical art workflows. Michaela’s role as art director and creative design lead on the experimental, environmental storytelling experience, “Hiraeth”, showcases the culmination of her expanded skillset in its utilization of state-of-art game technologies such as Unreal Engine 5, alongside a story grounded in the 5 beat story structure. Working alongside technical director, Brandon Figarella, programmer and level designer, Brandon Phuong, narrative lead, Lizzy Hatch, UI/UX designer, Michael Magsino, and audio lead, Miranda Palladino, Michaela hopes to continue to refine Hiraeth into a cohesive, poignant narrative of the importance of political resistance, environmental resilience and human connection up until graduation and even beyond. After completing her undergraduate degree, Michaela aspires to create interactive stories and worlds through digital media that examines the boundaries of technology and the arts. Moreover, she hopes to enter the industries of tech, entertainment design and art as a conscious creator who can look critically at the intersection of those spaces and work to help uplift underrepresented communities. She aims to accomplish this goal by continuing her studies after her undergraduate degree in post-graduate programs such as a masters and Ph.D in the fields of the digital arts and humanities. She believes that this path into interdisciplinary research will greatly aid their involvement with the larger technology and arts industries by equipping her to become a critical creative problem solver that pushes the boundaries of digital media in a holistic manner.
